# Decisions

Settled choices, with the reason. Append; do not rewrite history.

## 2026-09-05 (session 25 — جوهر، علت، معلول)

**گوهر is recommended for جوهر, and no word is coined.** Arabic *jawhar* is
Middle Persian *gōhr*; the L30 rule — where Persian has a good word, report
it — covers a loan-return as much as a native word. The technical compound
(جوهر فرد) is left to the linguist, because the corpus shows the poets kept
the Arabic there (35 lines to 3). Unconfirmed — L33.

**A cause/effect pair is coined as the two participles of one verb.** علّة /
معلول are one Arabic root; انگیزنده / انگیخته are V-022 and V-008 on انگیختن,
and the family (انگیزندگی، انگیزه، انگیزش) rebuilds entire. A pair split
across two roots would need two rulings and would not read as a pair.

**چرایی is offered for one sense of علت and not as half of the pair.** It is
classical and nominal (22 lines, فردوسی first) but it names *the reason why*
and has no partner for the effect. It cannot be built until چرا is a headword
(B7), and that is stated rather than worked around.

**شوند is not offered until L34 is ruled.** The engine cannot derive it;
offering it would mean handing the linguist a word on a rule he has not
written.

**A Behnevis capital is part of an `/explain` query.** Adjectives and
participles lowercase, nouns and action nouns capitalised. Recorded because
the first pass of this run misread four `rebuilt: false` answers that were
case mismatches; the protocol's verification step now says: query a candidate
in the case of the class you mean, and query both before calling it
unbuildable.

**Definition-derived candidates are recorded beside the lexical ones, not instead of them.** Aristotle's definitions were read and yield جنباننده / جنبانده (V-022 / V-008 on جنباندن — the efficient-cause pair, attested in مولانا's own statement of the argument) and زیرایستا (V-076 + V-024 — the substratum, the shape of *substantia*). They stay second because a definition reaches one sense of each term — the mover, what stands under — and the tradition's words were chosen precisely because they did not. Unconfirmed; L33 addendum.

## 2026-08-29 (session 24 — Windtelefon)

**بادگو is recommended for the wind phone, and the slot route is now a
precedent.** Where the front half of a concept is a concrete noun (اسم ذات),
the V-078/V-079 slot rules coin compounds today with no ruling — the سخنگو
shape, with بلندگو as the modern device-precedent. The verification is
forward-only (S32), and every L32-style report must say so.

**A candidate's obvious place-word gets its entry read before it is offered.**
بادگاه is Dehkhoda's word for a toilet. The cost of not checking would have
been the whole recommendation.

## 2026-08-25 (session 23 — امکان، ضرورت، واقعیت)

**Where a concept has a native verb, coin off the verb.** A مصدر is neither a
nām nor a kārnām, so L26 does not reach it and the V-rules apply: شایستن،
بایستن and کردن build 38 forms apiece where کار builds 1 and توان builds 2.
This is why L31's four terms are the first set in the project's history the
engine derives, and it is the first thing to try on the next term asked for.

**Tail counts decide compounds, not derivations.** karbon headwords compounds
(مرزبوم، سپهسالار، ساختمان) and does not headword regular deverbal derivations
(زندگی، دانش، کوشش، کردار، هستی، گفتمان — none is an entry, and دانش alone has
4,488 corpus occurrences). So a zero on ـستگی or ـایی measures editorial
policy. L29's ـبوم and L30's ـسالار stand; the method does not generalise past
compound tails, and that limit is now stated rather than discovered again.

**A gloss that lands exactly on a European philosophical term is a reason to
check provenance.** 132 lexicon entries mention دساتیر and 52 mention
آذرکیوان. ناور «ممکن… برابر واجب» and کنشمند «صاحب عمل… کننده و فاعل» are both
eighteenth-century forgeries and both were nearly recommended. Every سره
candidate now gets its entry read to the end before it is offered.

**واقعیت is kept out of the modal family on purpose.** Möglichkeit and
Notwendigkeit are modalities of one copula and Persian has two native modal
verbs for them; Wirklichkeit is ἐνέργεια against δύναμις, the other axis —
بالفعل/بالقوه, not the مواد ثلاث. Forcing a symmetric family would make Persian
assert Kant's classification over the one the language actually received.

## 2026-08-25 (session 22 — Demokratie)

**مردم‌سالاری stands; we do not offer a replacement.** It is wholly Persian,
established, understood, and on the only tail with real lexicon support —
ـسالار at 16 entries against ـشاهی at 5 and nothing at all on ـفرمانی or
ـتوانی. Where a language already has a good word, the coining protocol's
output is a question about it, not a rival for it.

**A candidate the engine derives is not thereby a candidate.** مردمگانی comes
back `rebuilt: true` on N-067 and was rejected anyway, because N-067's own
example خدایگانی is not a headword and the ـگانی tail carries grape varieties
and tanned leather. Buildability is a necessary test and never a sufficient
one — stated here because the بانگا precedent in L27 makes it easy to read
`rebuilt: true` as an endorsement.

**L26 and L28 are not one question and stop being carried as a pair.**
Rechtsstaat needs both rulings; Demokratie needs only L28, because مردم is
filed اسم ذات and داد is filed اسم معنی. The two are separable, they differ in
cost, and the linguist should be able to rule on either alone.

## 2026-08-24 (session 21 — a Persian word for Rechtsstaat)

**دادبوم is the recommended rendering, and the head was chosen on tail counts
rather than on the calque.** دادشهر is the closer copy of the German — شهر in
its Middle Persian sense of *realm* — but the lexicon has **3** entries ending
in ـشهر and all three are about the city, while **14** end in ـبوم and 12 are
اسم مرکب of the shape «[X]-land», one of which Dehkhoda glosses «کشور پاک».
The corpus agrees: ـشهر compounds there are town names. Recorded so the
recommendation is not re-argued from the calque alone. Unconfirmed — the
ruling is the linguist's, as L29.

**A candidate being free in the corpus is not a candidate being free.** The
corpus is classical and the terms are modern, so every surviving candidate is
also checked outside it before it is offered. دادمندی has 0 occurrences in
25,497,189 words and is nonetheless in modern use meaning عدالت; it was
withdrawn on that check alone.

**Rule of law, Rechtsordnung and Rechtsstaat are kept apart in the
candidates.** دادسالاری names a *rule* and دادسامان names an *order*; only
دادبوم/دادشهر/دادکشور name a **state**, which is what *Rechtsstaat* is a
predicate of. Offering the three as alternatives for one another would repeat
in Persian the conflation the German term exists to avoid.

## 2026-08-20 (session 20 — the tables are reversed, the affixes stop doubling, and the questions screen turns pages)

**The decomposer reverses the glide table, not a list of sound changes.**
`glides.unapply` is `glides.apply` read backwards, off the two shapes the table
itself has. Adding *y* between two vowels lived in one direction only, so the
engine built گوینده and then reported it as a word that cannot be built —
455 of 793 one-step glide forms over a 300-reading sample. Nothing about the
*y* is written out anywhere as Persian phonology; if the table changes, both
directions change with it.

**The round trip is a test, not a habit.** Every worked join in the glide
table is now asserted in both directions in the same parametrised list — what
the engine joins, it must be able to unjoin. A one-directional table is the
kind of fault that hides for as long as nobody happens to hand an output back
in as an input.

**Undoing a glide proposes; it never accepts.** A base reached by taking a
*y* back out has exactly the standing of every other proposal — it survives by
being in the lexicon and by rebuilding the word exactly. So the refusals hold:
*Mowlānā* and *Fanā* are still refused, and the cost of the extra proposals
(0.40s → 0.61s over six analyses) is paid in the rebuild, which is what the
rebuild is for.

**The search filters are a form, not a stack of boxes.** Each row is a label
in a fixed column and its controls in the next, so everything starts on one
line down the panel; every control has one height, one edge and one corner,
which meant taking the platform's own `select` chrome away with
`appearance: none` and drawing the arrow back; and the panel's ground sits
behind its explanation and its result rather than behind everything. The
project lead's word for the old one was that it looked like the eighties.

**The screen is Persian, and now the answer is too.** The ruling box had a
Behnevis field and an English field under it. Both were ours — the form the
project holds, and the language the repository is written in — and both sat
under the linguist's answer asking to be filled in. The *question* stopped
being shown three ways on 2026-08-16; the answer kept all three until
2026-08-20. `ruling_latin` and `ruling_en` stay in the store and in the export,
and a ruling that carries a Behnevis form still shows it. They are no longer
asked for.

**English evidence is not shown at all.** What a question carries under
«شواهد» was imported from our own Markdown, and it reached the screen as
left-to-right prose inside a right-to-left page, emphasis marks and all —
unreadable to the one person the screen exists for. Persian evidence still
shows; the **files** are the evidence a question actually turns on and they
were never the problem. The fold is not drawn when it would open onto nothing.

**A question's state is a badge, and why it was withdrawn is a sentence.** With
one question on screen at a time, whether *this* one is answered is the first
thing a reader needs and it was a small grey word at the end of the header. It
is now coloured by state and sits beside the reference. And «پس گرفته شده» had
no account behind it on screen although the store keeps one — `withdrawn_because`
is now printed.

**A ه standing after ا is never the silent one, whatever the Latin says.**
`join_fa` reads the Latin to tell بندگی from شاهگان, and that is right — the
Persian letter alone cannot separate them. But karbon writes a *pronounced*
final ه as **e** for **777 of the 803 readings ending in ـاه**, so the rule was
deleting a consonant and building شاگان، شاگین، شاگیدن: 28% of one-step forms
over a 40-reading sample. The one ending where the Persian settles it on its
own now overrules the Latin, because a silent ه writes a short *e* and a short
vowel cannot follow a long ā.

**Narrow on purpose, and it is not a workaround of the data.** کوه is *kuh* and
میوه is *mive*, both written وه, so nothing outside ـاه is guessed at — 204 ـوه
readings stay as karbon has them. And the reading itself is untouched: `Šāe` is
still what the lexicon hands us, still what the screens show, and
`/explain?word=گلوگاه` still fails. What changed is our orthography, not
karbon's value. The fault is UPSTREAM A9 with the numbers a pull request needs.

**A suffix may be added once per path, as a prefix already could.** The guard
was written for prefixes on 2026-08-15 and suffixes were left out on the
reasoning that *andišidegi* + *-i* is a real pattern — which is two different
suffixes, and nothing checked the other case. The engine built *Golstānstān*,
*Golzārzār*, *Golkadekade* and *goftee*: 2,725 of 161,774 forms over a
120-reading sample, 1,830 distinct spellings, **none of them in 25,497,189
tokens of corpus**. Settled by measuring, as the prefix half was, because
waiting is never the reason something stays wrong.

**Keyed on the affix, not the rule id — and here it matters more than it did
for prefixes.** N-093 and N-095 are both *-i*; N-074 and N-077 are both *-vār*.
A rule-id guard would have let one follow the other and built *Goliyi* and
*golvārvār* under two ids.

**What the guard is not.** It is not a ban on suffixing a suffixed word:
*Ravandegi* is *-ande* then *-gi* and is untouched. It is not about spelling
either — *nā-* in front and *-nā* behind are two affixes, and *Nāgolnā* still
builds. **Unconfirmed in one narrow place**: two different suffixes spelled
alike are refused together, which is the *andišidegiyi* case, and that stays
question L.

**The questions screen shows one question at a time.** Sixteen questions, each
several paragraphs with its files, its evidence and a box to answer in, was a
page minutes long with nothing on it saying where you were — the project lead
asked for next and previous on 2026-08-20. Next, previous, «پرسش ۳ از ۱۴», and
the bar repeated under the card because the moment you want the next question
is when you have read to the end of this one.

**Which question is open is in the address** — `/questions?q=L1`. A question is
a thing to point at, the same reason `/rules/{id}` has been a stable address
since the beginning.

**A question stays on screen when its own state changes underneath it.** The
screen opens on the unanswered; answering one makes it answered, and a filter
applied blindly would drop it and move the reader on, so the ruling just
written would vanish before it could be read back. It is held until the reader
moves. Choosing a different box clears the place, because that *is* the reader
asking to go somewhere.

**The last two `ch` line caps are gone.** `--measure` became the page width on
2026-08-18 on the project lead's own words — *do not add a line break when
there is enough space in the line* — but two screens kept a cap of their own:
the questions screen at 60ch and the word screen's definition at 62ch. A `ch`
is the width of the Latin zero, so 60ch is about 111 Persian characters, and
inside a card most of a 78rem page it broke every line at roughly half the
width. Neither has a cap now.

**An account carries `glide_id` beside `stem_form`.** The explain screen would
otherwise have shown «gu + -ande» with a letter appearing from nowhere. A
reader who cannot see where the *y* came from cannot check the account, which
is the whole purpose of the screen.

## 2026-08-18 (session 19 — the compound row shows its working, and six screens say it plainer)

**A compound row carries «راه ساخت»**: the front half and what the rule counted
it as, the behind half with **the infinitive it is a stem of**, what the two
make, and the rule's own sentence. The row named the parts and hid the rule,
so *where does دستور come from* was unanswerable on screen. The verb behind the
stem is the new fact — `Verb.infinitive` is carried for display only and never
used to build anything.

**Folded, not open.** Twenty rows of four lines is a scroll; checking one claim
is a deliberate act.

**The three counts, reordered.** «دستور می‌تواند ۴٬۶۵۲ ترکیب بسازد؛ پیکره ۱۰۹
تای آن را دارد. با ۱٬۷۴۱ واژهٔ دیگر سنجیده شد» → the corpus no longer *has*
them, they *appear in* it, and the third figure now says what it is: words
tried in the second position.

**The affix screen offers its vocabulary.** The meaning box accepted free text
over a closed set of 42 — a reader typing مکان got nothing and learned nothing.
Six buttons with their affix counts, plus a `datalist` of all 42. Same shape as
the example words on build and compound.

**The questions screen counts by state, and the counts are the filter.** One
checkbox became five boxes — همه، بی‌پاسخ، کار را متوقف کرده، پاسخ داده شده، پس
گرفته شده — over the store's own four states. **It does not invent a
*resolved* state**; that is a database change, not a screen one.

**The account id is off the questions screen.** «شما به‌عنوان expert-1 می‌توانید
پاسخ دهید» named a row in a table: one account, no roles since 2026-08-17. What
a reader can act on is whether answering is open.

**Two messages say it with an example instead of a term.** «الگوهای دو-بُنی بر
این واژه کار نمی‌کنند» asked a reader to know three words to learn one fact; it
now names گفت‌وگو and says how many rules ran and how many did not. The explain
screen's «هر یک از اینها دوباره ساخته شد» described the engine's own two-pass
check — propose backwards, rebuild forwards, keep what reproduces the spelling
— which is our business, not the reader's; it now says only that the routes
shown all reach this spelling and that meaning decides between them. Both were
reported not understood by the project lead on 2026-08-18.

**Question text is no longer justified.** Justification stretches word gaps to
force a flush edge, and on this measure that is what read as broken lines.

**The ⓘ occupies its own circle.** It was a 44px button pulled back by negative
margins on both sides, which collapses its footprint to nothing — so it printed
on top of whatever letters sat beside it. The box is the circle now; the touch
target is an absolutely-positioned overlay.

**A zero says why.** کار builds nothing — it is filed اسم معنی, which the
source grammar makes a کارنام, and all six slot rules take a نام — and the
screen answered with three figures about nothing («۰ ترکیب … ۰ تای آن … ۱٬۷۴۱
واژهٔ دیگر آزموده شد»). The route now serves both sides of the mismatch
(`word_classes`, `slot_wants`) and the screen writes the sentence from the
pack's own Persian, ending on the fact that the front slot's reach is an open
question (L18) rather than implying the word is at fault.

**The words nobody has written are reachable.** The compound list was the
attested one and nothing said so: دل shows 109 of 4,652, and the other 4,543 —
what this project exists to produce — could not be asked for from the screen at
all. Two buttons, counts on both, corpus first.

**A stem carries its infinitive.** «رو ۹۰٪ · آور ۹۰٪ · هز ۹۰٪» asked a reader
to place bare stems; it is «رو (رفتن)» now, from the same snapshot the stems
come from, and a nominal rule's bases show no brackets because they have none.
**And the rule is written out under it**, in the shape the step rows use — the
block said «دستور N-098» and left the reader to remember what that was.

**`npm run build` is the check, not `tsc --noEmit`.** The deployment build
failed on a type the loose check had passed — `TALLIES.find(...) ?? TALLIES[0]`
is possibly undefined under the app's own tsconfig, which `tsc -b` uses and a
bare `--noEmit` does not. Anything going to the server is built the way the
image builds it, first.

## 2026-08-18 (session 18, after — the stems filter as well as label)

**The two stems are the second filter.** A stem is where a word starts, so it
is the axis a reader wants beside the class — *show me only what گو makes*.
They were labels first; the project lead asked for filters, and he was right
the first time he raised it.

**Both are on when the screen opens**, and absent state means all: an answer is
not narrowed until a reader narrows it.

**The last one cannot be turned off.** An empty screen would say nothing about
why it is empty, so the final stem stays on and the press does nothing.

**Narrowed before grouping, so the class tiles recount.** A tile counting the
whole answer under a filter would put a number on itself that disagrees with
its own list. Checked against گفتن: both on gives کارنام ۹ / کارنده‌کاربهر ۸ /
… and turning گفت off gives ۵ / ۶ / … which sums to گو's 22.

**A word belongs to a stem if *any* of its routes starts there**, not just the
first. Measured on گفتن — 16 from گفت, 22 from گو, **none reachable from both**
— so on this data the two are a clean split and the parts add up to the whole.
Written for the general case anyway, because that is a fact about this word and
not about the grammar.

## 2026-08-18 (session 18, after — the search filters are open and the guide is an ⓘ)

**The filters are on the screen.** They were behind «ساختن جست‌وجو» and a
reader had to know to press it. The project lead: *the filters should be modern
and visible on search.* The builder still writes into the box and never runs
it, so nothing about *one language, one box* changes — only whether the reader
has to find it.

**And the screen still asks the API nothing until it is asked something.** That
rule was written when the panel was folded, and it survives unfolded: the word
classes are the one control needing `/rules`, and the list is fetched when a
reader first reaches that select — on focus or on a press — not on load. Two
tests hold it: one that `fetch` is untouched on arrival, one that reaching the
control loads the list.

**The class control renders before its list arrives, and leaves if the answer
is empty.** *Not asked yet* and *asked, and there are none* had to stop looking
alike: before the ask it must exist so a reader can reach it, and after an
empty answer it must go — an API a version behind serves no classes, and this
control losing itself quietly is what keeps it from taking the screen down.

**Information is an ⓘ, not a grey bar.** «راهنمای نحو» was a full-width panel
that read as an action being offered. It is now a small mark beside the field's
label. This is the same call as the example chips earlier the same day, and it
finishes it: what the app *offers* looks like a control, what the app *explains*
looks like an ⓘ.

**A control is never narrower than its longest word.** The option text is
phrases — «پایان‌یافته به», «نابِسودکارواژه», «واژه‌نامهٔ کاربن» — so the selects
size to their own content rather than to the column. Checked in the browser at
1280px and 375px with the longest option selected in each: nothing clipped,
and no sideways scroll.

## 2026-08-18 (session 18, last — the row says its four parts, and the screen shows its stems)

**The row states the score's four parts, in words.** FRONTEND.md has always
required *its parts, never one number*, and three of four reached the screen
while the fourth — rule productivity — was computed, sent, used to sort, and
never shown. The row now carries: what the corpus has, what the middle step
stands on, what each rule built and how much of it was found, and what the
base means against the bases its rule takes.

**No rate is shown where a count will do.** The project lead's objection stands
and is the rule from here: *a reader cannot read a percentage whose method they
were never told.* So «۸۵٪ — زیاد» becomes the five bases the figure was measured
against with the base placed among them, and the chain's median realisation
becomes each rule's own «۵٬۱۹۶ ساخته، ۳۵ پیدا شد». The median was chosen to rank
a list and is true of no rule in it: وارفته's two steps realise 0.67% and 9.18%.

**Never «هیچ‌کس ننوشته».** A draft of the middle-step sentence said nobody had
written the intermediate form. FRONTEND.md forbids *does not exist*, and the
corpus is classical poetry — a word absent from it may be ordinary prose. Every
claim now names the corpus version. The rule applies to written interface copy,
not only to the evidence line.

**The two stems are named, and counted.** «بر پایهٔ raft row» became کاربن یک
and کاربن دو, each with its Persian, its Latin and how many words start there —
16 from رفت and 22 from رو for رفتن. The split was measured all along and
thrown away, on a screen belonging to a project named after kārbon.

**The classes are a grid of tiles, and the words sit below it.** Stacked
full-width rows ran to ~1,100px of index before a word appeared, and a folded
class and an open word were the same shape. A tile has a surface and a border;
a word row does not. **The words render below the whole grid**, never inside a
tile: the first attempt let the opened tile span the grid and pushed the other
five classes below a screenful of words, which is the fault being fixed rather
than a smaller version of it. One class open at a time, marked on its tile.

**A step shows what it makes.** `raft + vā- ← vāraft`. The chain was there and
the arrow was missing; a reader had to work out that each step's base was the
one above's output. Derived, not sent: a step's result is the next step's base.

## 2026-08-18 (session 18, after — a route says which one it is and what it means)

**A step carries the sense its rule holds.** `/generate` sends `tags_fa` on
every worded step: the tag's own `gloss_fa`, read from the rule pack and never
written in the API. Without it the four routes to وارفتنی printed the same
sentence four times, because `explanation_fa` describes mechanics and the
mechanics are the one thing those four rules share.

**Only on the worded shape.** `worded=false` exists so that بلند's 8,731 steps
stay readable, and a meaning is wording. A test asserts the unworded step does
not carry it.

**An empty list, never a missing key.** A rule with no tag sends `[]`. A caller
should not have to tell *no sense* from *this build is older than the field*.

**Every route is numbered and boxed.** «راه ۱ از ۴». Four routes in one panel
separated by a gap read as one list of twelve rules — which is exactly what the
project lead reported. Numbering only appears where there is more than one
route, because «راه ۱ از ۱» is noise.

**What this does not decide.** Four rules that attach the same prefix and
differ only in meaning may be right or may be one rule with four senses. That
is the linguist's, and the same question as S26 on the compound screen. The
display now shows the difference either way, so it does not depend on the
answer.

## 2026-08-18 (session 18, last — the copy button goes)

**A built word has two actions, not three.** «رونوشت» — which copied the
Persian form to the clipboard — is removed on the project lead's word: not
necessary. What is left is «راه ساخت», which opens the chain of rules that
produced the word, and «داوری», which is how an expert rules on it. Explain and
judge; taking is what a reader's own selection already does.

It was there for a stated reason — *translators paste these into their own
text* — so this is a decision rather than a cleanup, and it reverses that one.

**One thing it took with it, caught in the browser and not by the tests.** The
button shared its whole declaration with the expander: the CSS read
`.toggle, .copy { … }`. Removing the copy half left `.toggle` with nothing but
`color: var(--ink-faint)`, so «راه ساخت» stopped being a touch-sized control in
the accent colour and became a line of faint text — and every test still
passed, because none of them asserts a colour or a height. Restored, and the
rule now names one selector.

## 2026-08-18 (session 18, later still — the build screen leads with its classes)

**A word class is closed until it is asked for.** The build screen showed the
classes and then opened all of them, which made the grouping invisible: 660
words in 8 classes per reading, 47,802px of page. It now shows the eight
classes with their counts and what each one means, and opens the one the reader
presses. The project lead: *the reader sees a long list although we have
categories; she should see the categories first.*

**Nothing is dropped, and the twenty-row fold inside a group stays.** An opened
group still starts at twenty and says on the control how many it holds back —

## 2026-08-17 (session 17, later — the app explains itself)

## 2026-08-17 (session 16, later — publishing and the credential)

**Murfulu is published at `murfulu.mosaic-hub.de`, through a Cloudflare Tunnel
rather than a server.** The sibling apps on this domain already work that way —
`sokhan` runs from a workstation and reaches the internet through `cloudflared`
— and following the pattern removed the whole server question rather than
answering it. Three consequences and none is a downgrade: Cloudflare terminates
TLS instead of Caddy, `cloudflared` creates the DNS record instead of a person,
and **ports 80 and 443 are not open at all**.

**Caddy binds to `127.0.0.1` and the site address carries its scheme.** Both
were wrong for this shape and both were quietly unsafe: Caddy asked Let's
Encrypt for a certificate it could never validate behind a tunnel, and it
published on `0.0.0.0`, which on a workstation offers the corpus to everything
on the same wifi as well as to `cloudflared`. `MURFULU_BIND` and
`MURFULU_SITE_ADDRESS` exist for exactly this, and a server sets them the other
way.

**One account, one password, no keys.** A reader needed two credentials to be
useful — the site password to see anything and an expert API token to answer
anything — and the project lead's call is that this is one too many for a
research app with one reviewer. Migration `0008` drops `api_tokens` and the
`role` column; the wall in front of the whole site is the authentication, and
everyone behind it may grade a word, propose a rule change and settle a
question. This reverses PLAN 6.3, which built the roles, and it is the second
time this project has removed a role rather than added one.

**`users` stays, with one row, and that is the deliberate half.** Feedback,
rulings and proposals point at an author and are append-only precisely so a
judgement keeps whoever made it; dropping the column would have discarded that
on nine stored records and made a change of mind an excavation rather than a
migration. The account **adopts the oldest existing one** instead of adding
itself beside it, so records made either side of the upgrade point at the same
author and the history does not split at it.

**What it costs is inter-rater agreement, and it is written down in three
places rather than one.** REVIEW.md's whole argument for storing feedback is
that it becomes the set the scoring weights are fitted against, and its export
carries a per-author pseudonym so two reviewers' verdicts on one word can be
compared. With one identity there is only ever one rater. Records made before
today keep their own authors and stay comparable; the dataset manifest says so
in the field's own description, because a reader outside this repository has no
other way to find out.

**`author_role` is removed from the export rather than left as a constant.** A
constant in a research dataset reads as a distinction somebody measured.

**`may_leave_a_verdict` stays, and is now always true.** A client that stopped
asking could not be told *no* again without being rewritten, and the sentence
*who may grade a word* should live in one place. This project added a third
role and removed it a day later, so the reversal is not hypothetical.

**Elasticsearch gets a snapshot repository, in both compose files.** Not a
preference: `docs/DEPLOY.md` moves the corpus to a server by snapshot rather
than by ingest, and Elasticsearch refuses to register any `fs` repository while
`path.repo` is empty — so the first command of that runbook could not run. A
separate volume from `es_data`, because a snapshot is wanted exactly when the
cluster is being wiped.

## 2026-08-17 (session 16 — the open-questions sweep)

**A cached derivation re-derives its presentation; it does not store it.**
S1 asked whether the stored path should grow the three fields `/generate` shows
and the index does not keep — the base at each step, the *ow → av* stem form,
and the glide alternatives — or whether a cache hit should rebuild them in
memory and use the index only for the evidence. **The second.** Measured on
derivations-v10: the three fields add **245 bytes a record, 18.6%**, taking the
index from 2.28 GB to 2.71 GB and needing a full rebuild to land. Re-deriving
them costs **0.02s at two steps and 0.48s at three**.

**Size is the smaller half of the reason.** The three fields are deterministic
functions of the rule pack, and the pack is versioned. Storing them freezes a
presentation against the pack that produced it, so a `glide_alternatives`
written under 0.1.0 and read under 0.2.0 would be a true-looking figure under a
false label — the failure this project keeps finding, most recently in the two
screens quoting different corpus versions. Re-deriving cannot go stale.

**ARCHITECTURE constraint 6 is met either way**, which is what makes this a
choice rather than a rule. *Every generated word carries its derivation* — and
the stored path already carries the rule ids, the affixes and the glide ids.
What is absent is presentation detail, not derivation.

**A live result is never written into the `derivations` index.** S2 asked how
one could reach it without damage, and both offered answers damage something:
writing by the same id replaces a merged record — 439,078 words are built more
than one way — with a single-source one, and writing only what is absent leaves
the cache unable to find the older forms again.

**So it does not go there at all.** The `derivations` index is a **release
artefact**: it is versioned, built as a batch, compared version against version
by `tools/compare_versions.py`, and *nothing else moved* is a property a
release is checked against. A live write makes that property untestable and the
index unreproducible — drift caused by whoever happened to browse. The project
has met this exact shape before: three rulings were edited into generated rule
files instead of into `rules/decisions.yaml`, and re-running the builder
reverted all three without a word.

A live cache is therefore its own index beside the release one, read after it
and never merged into it. It is disposable by design: dropping it costs the
1.3 seconds a deep request already takes, not a rebuild.

**The read half of S2 was already built** and the entry had not caught up.
`murfulu/search/attested.py` asks `word_types` in one batched query and fills
the same `Index` class the batch job fills, so the two cannot drift about what
counts as attested.

**A form that also reads as an inflection ranks below one that does not.**
S8's second half, which the entry said no condition over the fields we hold
could fix. It is fixed without those fields: the inflection analyser built in
session 10 to *measure* the problem is now asked at request time. On رفتن at
two steps, **seven of the top twenty read as inflections before and none does
now** — رفتی moves from 12 to 57, رفتار from 13 to 10, روش from 16 to 13.

**Both the clear and the shared cases are demoted**, and that is the part worth
recording. 95.6% of inflection-shaped forms are spelled by a derivational rule
too, so demoting only the clear ones would leave رفتی above رفتار — the
complaint itself — untouched, because *-ی* is also N-095. The letters cannot
settle which reading is meant and the row says so, naming the rules. Marked and
ranked down, never dropped: a word the corpus wrote is evidence whatever it is
evidence of.

**A deep form keeps the score measured on its first step.** S15 asked whether
it should instead be scored on its own outer rule. **No.** A base's vector is
the average of corpus lines using the word, so an unwritten intermediate —
*yāni* — can never have one, and producing one is FRACSS's job and is
unvalidated on this material: re-measured on v10, **79.0% of attested pairs
have a bound stem as their base**, up from 76.8%. It is also no longer needed.
The realisation figure orders deep answers, which is the job typicality was
being asked to do and cannot — at three steps 12,999 forms share 22 typicality
values between them.

**The three semantic measures will be built, and not yet.** S6 asked whether it
is acceptable to build all three and let the expert feedback set choose. It is —
but that set (8.3) does not exist, and re-measured on v10 only **65 of 195
rules have any attested pair and 23 have 200 or more**, so a three-way
comparison today would be decided on a tenth of the pack. Parts 1 and 2 stand
as recommended: script-keyed public vectors, BGE-m3 (MIT).

**`rule_chain` is renamed `built_by`.** S19. It became the union of every path
in 2026-08-14 and the name went on saying chain. Nothing read it, so the rename
was free, and no alias is kept — an alias would preserve the trap. It takes
effect at the next corpus version.

**`explained` is renamed `rebuilt`, and nothing learns to judge meaning.** S21.
The flag means only that a chain of real rules reproduces the letters from a
dictionary entry, and it came back true for *Nāxodāgāhi* on ناخدا, a ship's
captain. Refusing that would need a ruling this project does not make in code,
so the name is corrected and the **base's own corpus evidence** is served
beside every account instead. It does part of the job and not all of it, which
is stated: *Hamzamāni*'s base has 1 document and ناخدا has 213.

**`/explain` accepts a Persian spelling, by looking it up.** S11. Every reading
is analysed, not the first — کنار is *Kanār*, *Kenār* or *Konār* and the
dictionary commits to none. Never transliterated: guessing the Behnevis without
diacritics measured 5.8% correct. A spelling the lexicon does not hold is still
refused, and the message now gives that as the reason. Measured on 20 derived
words a reader would plausibly paste, the lexicon holds 9 — this halves the
refusals and does not remove them.

**An unfinished search is not a refusal.** Found chasing S16's claim that
درافکندگی is the engine's largest gap. It is not a missing rule: the engine
stops at 20,000 forms per base, building forward from افکندن reaches 20,050 at
three steps, and the search never arrives. `/explain` now serves `settled`, and
measured over 120 random readings **5.8% of empty answers at three steps and
7.5% at four were unfinished searches rather than refusals**. مولانا remains a
settled refusal, which is what the whole check exists for.

**The coining prototype waits for L10.** S16. Both engineering reasons its
proposals failed are now gone — compounds reach a reader, and an unfinished
search says so — but the wall has not moved: five affixes still hand back one
identical meaning, so a model would choose between them on its own priors,
which is the exact failure the design exists to avoid. When it is built it must
call `/explain` on its own proposals and read `settled` as well as `rebuilt`.

**The project's own grammar terms are data.** S3. کارنام, برنام, کاربن دو and
the rest lived only in a design document, so every screen labelled its groups
اسم مصدر — the interface naming its own output in the vocabulary the project
exists to replace. They are authored in `rules/word_classes.yaml` and served
from `/rules`. Four terms and all twelve explanations are ours and are marked
unconfirmed; the vocative gets **no** coined term, because *Nedāyi* is itself
Arabic-derived and writing ندایی there would have hidden the gap. See L17.

**A prefix may be added once per path, and the corpus said so rather than the

Approved by the project lead 2026-08-15, the day it was found. آبرو is *Ābrav*

**Upstream work goes as issues, never as pull requests.** The project lead's

are written out in `build/upstream/ISSUES-for-karbon.md` in non-technical
language, each with its own context, worked examples, the attachment it needs,
and how the number was measured — so the reader can act without this repository
or this conversation.

**The linguist answers in the app, and the app is the record.** The project
lead's decision, 2026-08-15, on both halves: a **separate** flow from expert
review, and the ruling given there **becomes the record** — which turns
`docs/FOR_THE_LINGUIST.md` into an export rather than the place answers are
written down.

**Separate flow, same two roles.** The *flow* is separate because the two hold
different things: a graded verdict is one opinion about one built word and
dozens are aggregated into evidence, while a ruling settles a question about the
grammar once and the code changes. **A third role was built for that on
2026-08-15 and taken out again on 2026-08-16**, before anything was committed.

The argument for it was real — an expert grades, the linguist settles, and with
one role any expert can overwrite a ruling. The argument against it won: **in
practice the expert is the linguist**, and a concept that names the same person
twice costs a role, a permission check, a migration and a paragraph of
explanation to buy a distinction nobody in the project needs. The project lead's
call. Settling a question is now an expert's right, like leaving a verdict and
proposing a rule change.

**And the karbon maintainer gets no role, deliberately.** They never sign in.
Feedback about the lexicon is already tagged `owner: karbon` on the way in and
leaves by `tools/export_feedback.py --owner karbon`, to be opened as an issue on
their own tracker. The asymmetry with the linguist is the point: karbon has a
repository and an issue tracker where an answer belongs beside the code it is
about, and the linguist has neither — his answers had nowhere to live but an
English Markdown file he does not read. That gap is what the app fills.

**Why the store is the record here and the YAML stays the record for
proposals.** They hold different things. A proposal is a change to something
that already exists and can be reviewed as a git diff; a ruling is new
information that exists nowhere until he gives it, exactly like the expert
feedback table. So `linguist_rulings` is append-only in the same way and for
the same reason — a later ruling that reverses an earlier one is a new row
saying so, and on Postgres a trigger refuses every UPDATE and DELETE.

**The document still exists, and is generated.** Two reasons, neither of them
habit: a question and its ruling belong in the git history beside the code they
justify, so a reader six months later finds the reason without a database; and
the repository is what gets reviewed, so a decision only a running service can
show is a decision nobody reviews. `tools/export_rulings.py` writes it.

**Seeding never invents the Persian.** The document is in English and he works
in Persian. Translating a question automatically on the way in would make our
English the original, which is the exact failure this change exists to fix — so
seeding carries the English across, marks every question that still needs its
Persian wording, and the export counts them at the top.

longer ranks a form**, case folded so `Goft` and `goft` stay one word. The
number stays on screen with its caveat; only the ranking stops using it.
What this does **not** reach is recorded in S8: where the lexicon knows no
reading at all, nothing separates رفتی from گفتار.

**A character outside the Behnevis alphabet is repaired, not overridden.**
`murfulu/lexicon/behnevis.py` maps look-alikes back as readings are read. An
override records a value this project *believes differently* and is
unconfirmed until the linguist rules; nobody believes a Greek omicron. Only
look-alikes: تباهیدن's Arabic ه is left as the snapshot has it and flagged,
because what was meant is a guess. Reported upstream as A5 and goes away when
it lands there.

**A rule that stops applying must say why, in the pack.** `Rule` grew
`retired_reason` and the loader refuses a retirement without one — the
guarantee `Glide` has had since G-01. Ids are permanent and a retired rule is
still served, so the reason is the only thing separating a ruling from a rule
somebody switched off. **The reason is not shown to readers**: every one is
written in English and the interface may not invent Persian. See S14.

**A long list opens at twenty and says what it is holding.** Both the build
groups and the rules list. Nothing is hidden — the count of the folded rows is
on the control that opens them, and one press shows the lot. The property that
matters is that the page length is set by how many groups or families there
are rather than by how many rows were built, so it stops growing with the
answer.

**Glide rules are served in their own shape**, `kind: "glide"` with `left`,
`right`, `result` — not as affix rules with the affix fields null, which would
put blanks on screen where an affix goes. The pack total shown on screen is
therefore 212, which is what the pack has always held; 195 was the count with
the glides silently dropped.

**A prop-derived draft is synchronised in an effect, not during the render.**
React documents the render-time adjustment and it does not survive here: the
router navigates inside a transition, and an adjustment made during one can be
discarded when the transition replays. Measured — the browser test failed every
run on the render-time version. Applies to both search boxes.

## 2026-08-13 (session 8)

**The Persian side of a join is spelled as Persian spells it, unconfirmed.**
A base whose silent ه the Latin writes as *e* loses it before a گ: تازه + ی is
تازگی. Standard orthography, and the source grammar's own glide examples
(*Tāzegi*, *Mordegān*) already assume it, so this follows the working rule —
build it now, mark it unconfirmed, register it (FOR_THE_LINGUIST L7). **Silent
is decided by the Latin**, never by the letter, so شاه keeps its ه.
The neighbouring case — the ZWNJ inside a compound, item K — is deliberately
**not** built: it needs the Behnevis joining specification, and this one did
not.

**Superseded corpus versions are dropped once a new one is serving.** Settled
by the project lead. v1 and v2 were deleted after v3 went live — 926 MB.
`IndexAdmin.drop` stays deliberate and is still never part of a release, so
dropping is a decision somebody makes rather than something a pipeline does.
What it costs is worth stating once: `documents-v1` was the only way to re-run
the stopword comparison, so the *finding* (0 poems against 10,636) survives in
the docs and the *ability to re-measure it* does not.

**Persian text is analysed without a stopword list, in an analyser of our own.**
Lucene's stock `persian` analyzer removes رفت، گفت، شد، بود — a relevance
device, and here every word is evidence. Measured: a phrase query for رفت
returned 0 poems from a corpus writing it 17,499 times. The same analyser also
drops the character filter that splits a word at the zero-width non-joiner,
because `wordtypes` counts می‌رود as one word and the two must agree about
what a word is.

**A doubted claim is ranked last, never deleted.** The homograph pass measures
a claim's meaning against its rule and its base, and a cosine is evidence about
a claim rather than a ruling on it. Doubted sisters sink and stay visible;
doubted claims are held out of `word_types.rule_ids` — which is an assertion —
and recorded in the affix map's `contested` list, so the count is readable
either way. — follows the standing rule: nothing is hidden, only ranked.

**Where a measurement needs a cut, the cut comes from a null the data
supplies.** Both homograph checks are ranks rather than cosines, so the null is
uniform and the cut is where the claim density falls to it. The first attempt
used an absolute cosine and flagged 100% of claims; that failure is what
settled the method.

**A stored derivation may answer *not established*.** The grounding question
needs the forms between the base and the word, and a stored path keeps only the
base of its last step. Rather than store more (which is open question S1) or
answer wrongly, a path too deep to read comes back `None`. A stored `False`
would say the corpus was asked and answered no.

**Every corpus change is a new version, even when the counts do not move.**
v2 is the analyser; v3 is the cleaned affix map. The word counts are identical
across all three, and the indices are not, so the version is what the indices
say rather than what the corpus text says.

## 2026-08-12 (session 5)

**The derivation cache lives in Elasticsearch only.** ARCHITECTURE listed a
`derivations` table under Postgres and also said live results are cached in
the `derivations` index. Settled by its own rule of thumb: a cache is
rebuildable, so it is not authored data. No table was created.

**`expert_feedback` is append-only in the database.** A trigger refuses every
UPDATE and DELETE. It is the only human work here that no job can recreate,
and it doubles as the evaluation set, so the guarantee is enforced rather than
agreed.

**A linguistic verdict may only come from an expert, and is refused at the
store, not at the route.** The rule holds however the record arrives — API,
script or test. Task 6.3 still decides who the caller is.

**A graded verdict and the `linguistic` category imply each other.** One check
constraint in both directions, so a bug report cannot carry a verdict and a
linguistic judgement cannot go ungraded.

**The `rules` table mirrors the loaded rule, field for field.** The singular
`input_type` / `semantic_tag` / `example` in the architecture sketch predate
the rule pack and would have dropped data on the way in.

**Rule ids are never deleted by the loader.** An id in the database the pack
no longer mentions is reported and left alone. Ids are permanent; one that
disappeared is a fault, not a tidy-up.

**No credentials are stored yet.** `users` holds the role. Choosing an
authentication scheme belongs to task 6.3, not to the schema.

**Authentication is an API token, not a password.** The app is API-first and
local. A token is issued once, pasted in, and revoked when done with; nothing
holds a password, resets one or expires a session. A login screen can later
sit on top of the same accounts.

**Only the SHA-256 of a token is stored, with no stretcher.** A token is 256
bits of operating-system randomness — not guessable, not reused anywhere — so
stretching would cost without adding safety. The reasoning is written beside
the code because it stops holding the moment a human-chosen secret is stored.

**A token is revoked, never deleted; an account is disabled, never deleted.**
Feedback is append-only and names its author, so a credential or an account
that vanished would leave records pointing at nothing.

**The author of a feedback record comes from the token, never from the
request body.** The body cannot choose an author, so it cannot choose the
wrong one.

**A migration never imports a model.** Allowed values are written out as
literals, so a later model edit cannot silently rewrite what an old migration
did.

**The frontend is Vite + React + TypeScript in strict mode**, with Vitest and
Testing Library. Strict, because the Python side is `mypy --strict` and one
half of a project checking less than the other is where the bugs collect.

**Plain CSS with custom properties, no framework.** The tokens *are* the design
system, and a test fails if a stylesheet names a token nobody defined or writes
a colour that is not one. A framework would put the design decisions somewhere
nobody on this project can read.

**Fonts are self-hosted from npm, never a CDN.** The app is private and local;
a font CDN would leak every visit and break with no network.

**Logical properties only — no `left` or `right` in any stylesheet**, enforced
by a test. A right-to-left layout built by mirroring a left-to-right one breaks
the first time a component is nested inside another.

**No hamburger menu.** Six items fit in a scrolling row at 375 px. Hiding the
navigation would make an app that has to teach its own vocabulary harder to
learn.

**The app calls `/api` on its own origin, and never names a host.** The dev
server proxies it; nginx proxies it in the container. Where the API lives is
deployment, not code.

**A test may not delete what it did not create, and must put back any alias
it moves.** Running the suite on a machine with a live cluster destroyed an
ingested corpus. Fixture cleanup is now scoped by a prefix the tests own, and
aliases are saved and restored.

**Facets are aggregations over the whole result set, never counts of the
returned page.** A page count changes with the page size and looks exactly like
a real figure.

**A facet narrows the query in the box rather than filtering behind it.** One
way to express a search, and the syntax is learned by using the interface.

**Query syntax is shown left to right, as it must be typed.** `dir="auto"`
puts the asterisk on opposite sides for `*nā` and `*نا`, and one of them would
be copied wrongly.

**Layout is tested in a real browser, not in jsdom.** jsdom has no layout
engine, so a component test cannot see a row overflowing a phone screen or a
touch target that came out 31 px tall — and those are the failures a reader on
a phone hits first. Playwright runs every screen at 375 px and at desktop
width. It found two bugs on its first run.

**The `web` container is built in Node and served by nginx.** The running image
carries no build tools and no source, and nginx supplies the two things a
single-page app needs: a fallback to `index.html` for deep links, and the API
proxy.

## 2026-08-11 (session 4)

**Only a confirmed reading may claim that a rule formed a corpus word.** An
attested spelling the lexicon has no entry for is kept as a *proposal* and
never written into the index. Measured: claiming those too attached the
Persian suffix *-nā* to مولانا، نخلنا and eighty more Arabic phrases ending in
the possessive نا. The index asserts; the proposal list asks.

**The affix pass writes a new corpus version, never the live index.** It runs
after the precompute, hands its result to the ingest as a file, and the alias
moves once when the version is whole.

**The precompute merges across the whole lexicon, in memory.** The stored id is
the Latin form plus the output class, so a form two lexicon entries reach is
one document with two derivations. Streaming the write and letting
Elasticsearch key on that id would have silently kept only the last —
439,078 of 1.7 million words are built more than one way. The cost is 4.2 GB
of memory for one run, which is acceptable for a batch job and is measured.

**The merge is keyed on the whole path, not the rule chain.** 95,021 forms are
reached by the same rule from two different entries. A chain-only key dropped
the second while the record still named a single source.

**Two Persian spellings under one Behnevis reading both stay on the record.**
10,270 words are affected. Which one the app shows is a linguistic question
(OPEN_QUESTIONS Q2) and it is not settled by choosing a dedup key.

**A rule that never fired has no productivity, not a productivity of zero.**
29 rules cannot be exercised by this pass — stem rules are a different
operation, compounding is generated on demand, and four rules need word
classes the pool has none of. Each carries its reason. A rule that *did* fire
and has no attested output is measured at zero, which is a result.

**The realisation rate is the primary productivity measure; Baayen's P is
stored beside it.** Measured median P is 0.0069, too small to rank anything,
because it divides hapaxes by tokens and a rule's attested output is dominated
by a few very frequent words. The realisation rate — attested built forms over
all built forms, Monakhov's simulate-and-check measure — is what this pipeline
was shaped for. P is kept because the literature expects it.

**A form identical to its own input is counted as an echo, not as evidence.**
Five rules score near the top of the ranking because their output is the same
string as their base — three zero-affix rules and *-an*, which rebuilds the
infinitive the run started from. Any later scoring must exclude them.

**Sister forms are ordered by evidence, not frequency.** A confirmed reading
outranks a bare spelling, and document count outranks occurrence count.
*Goftār* at 4,813 occurrences sits below *Kaftār* at 75, because the lexicon
confirms how *Kaftār* is read and has no entry for *Goftār*.

**`/search` searches `word_types` and takes no index parameter.** Every field
the query language names is a `word_types` field, so offering another index
would let a query that can never match look like one that found nothing.

**Nothing invents a store to make a route exist.** `/feedback` is append-only
in Postgres by design; there is no Postgres layer, so the route is absent and
the missing layer is a plan task.

## 2026-08-11 (session 3)

**There is no `D` family. All 81 verb-table rows are V-001 … V-081.** The
planned denominal-verb family never existed in any source. The concept document
names exactly two rules that make a verb from a noun — `-idan` and `-āndan` —
and those are verb rows 3 and 4, now V-003 and V-004 with a
`makes_verb_from_nominal` flag. Rows 1 and 2 make a verb from a verb stem, so
grouping all four together would have mixed two operations. Same principle the
family table already applies to compounding templates: a rule belongs to the
family of the table it comes from.

**`S-01` … `S-09` mirror the stem document's own rule numbers**, and its
sub-rules 4-1 and 4-2 are parts of S-04 rather than rules of their own. The
document says stem 2 divides into "eight groups", which is rules 2–9.

**The rule pack is generated, not hand-written.** `tools/build_rule_pack.py`
rebuilds the YAML from the raw transcriptions plus two authored tables. Hand
edits would drift from the source and could not be re-checked; this way the
transcription and the judgement calls are separate files, and the judgement
calls are the small ones a linguist can read.

**`label_fa` and `explanation_fa` are composed from the grammar's Persian type
names, not authored per rule.** 212 hand-written Persian sentences would be
212 chances to introduce an error, and the composition reproduces the wording
already in RULE_PACK.md. The *gloss templates* on the semantic tags stay
hand-authored, because those state meaning rather than structure.

**A V or N rule id carries the source document's own row number.** V-010 is
verb row 10. The alternative — sequential ids independent of the document —
was rejected because the three worked examples in RULE_PACK.md already assume
the correspondence, and because it lets the linguist be shown his own row
number when reviewing.

**Rows the document left unnumbered are appended, not inserted.** V-082 and
N-105 sit at the end of their family even though they belong beside rows 8 and
84 in the document. Follows directly from the append-never-insert rule. Their
document position is kept in a `follows_row` field so nothing is lost.

**An id for a blank source row is reserved, never reused.** Verb row 77 is
empty, so V-077 exists in the register with a reason and no rule. Cheaper than
a renumbering later.

**The lexicon snapshot id is the file name plus a hash of its contents**, not
its modification time. Copying the same snapshot to another machine must give
the same id, or figures computed on one machine cannot be compared with figures
computed on another.

**The shared corpus-adapter test lives in the package, not in `tests/`.** A new
adapter proves itself by calling `check_adapter`, so the second corpus meets
the same bar as the first without anyone having to remember what it was.

**An adapter must be repeatable.** Two passes must yield the same documents in
the same order. Ingest is re-run on every corpus rebuild, and an adapter that
reorders makes two corpus versions incomparable.

**Unmapped is a stated position, not a missing value.** Asking for the word
classes of a category we deliberately did not map raises, rather than returning
an empty result. Otherwise "we decided not to" and "there happen to be none"
look identical at the call site.

**`is_compound` is carried on the category mapping, not modelled as a word
class.** Whether an already-compound entry may feed the compounding rules is a
separate open question; keeping the flag lets that be decided later without
redoing the mapping.

## 2026-08-11

**Murfulu works on the Latin (Behnevis) level, not the Arabic-script level.**
The word-formation rules key on final vowels and consonants and insert glides
between vowels. Arabic script does not write short vowels, so the rules cannot
be applied reliably from it. karbon converts to Latin first.

**Latin convention: the later one** — `š ž č j ˈ`, from
`قواعد_مصدر_و_لاتین‌نویسی` (14 May 2026), not the earlier `c dj v` set in
`درآمد_پروژه`. Reason: it matches the linguistics-site standard so data can be
matched against outside sources, and karbon already implements it.

**Generation is Murfulu's job; karbon stays as it is.** karbon covers Persian →
Latin, the lexicon, and stem extraction, and is under active review with the linguist.
We do not fork it or change its rules.

**The audience is writers, translators and thinkers — not everyday speakers.**
A generated word does not have to catch on. It has to be correctly formed and
precisely defined. This is stated across the concept documents and settles most
"but would anyone say that?" arguments.

**Every generated word must carry its derivation.** From the *sāzāyi* incident:
the system must be able to state which rule produced a form and from which
stem. Output without a derivation path is a defect, not a stylistic choice.

## 2026-08-11 (architecture)

**Frontend is a React SPA** in its own container; the backend is API-only.

**karbon is imported as a library** (`karbon-nlp`), not called over HTTP.
Murfulu pins a karbon version and mounts the lexicon file locally.

**Rule pack is YAML in git, loaded into Postgres.** YAML is the source of
truth — reviewable and diffable. Postgres is the query layer.

**Postgres for authored data**, Elasticsearch for anything rebuildable by a
batch job.

**Blocking is not a scoring penalty.** An existing word covering the same
meaning is shown as related information only. Filling gaps convention left is
the point of the project.

**Corpus-agnostic from day one.** Ganjoor is source #1, not the design target.
Books and prose follow, so nothing may assume poetry.

**Search by semantic tag is a first-class feature**, not a later addition. It

## 2026-08-11 (generation behaviour)

**Depth is not a quality limit.** A three-step form is not worse for being
three steps. Generating new words is the point of the project, so the chain is
not cut short to be safe. Generation runs until no rule matches. — project lead,
correcting an earlier draft that capped depth at 2.

**Two mechanical limits remain:** a hard stop at 5 steps with cycle detection
(several rules return the type they consume, so chains can run forever), and a
storage limit — steps 1–2 are precomputed, deeper is generated on demand.

**Depth does not lower the score.** Each step carries its own rule strength and
they multiply, so a chain containing a weak rule fades by itself. No separate
depth penalty.

**Live results are cached** in the `derivations` index, tagged with the corpus
version. The first request pays; later ones are lookups. The cache grows into
the precompute.

**One row per word.** Grouping key is `form_latin` + `output_type` — not the
Persian spelling, not case-folded. Alternative derivation paths are kept and
shown nested, never discarded: two paths for one form often means two senses.

**Numbers are shown in Persian digits (۱۲۳).**

**All seven screens are built, not a subset.**

## 2026-08-11 (quality bar)

**The frontend must be genuinely good, not functional-only.** Mobile first,
responsive, minimal in the Apple sense — space over lines, one accent colour,
typography for hierarchy, progressive disclosure. The app has to teach its own
vocabulary, because nobody knows what *kārnām* means. WCAG 2.1 AA.

**Standard engineering practice throughout.** Dockerised, modular, tested,
documented. **No file over 400 lines** — karbon's 1,257-line phonemizer with six
near-duplicate versions is the specific failure we are avoiding. See
ENGINEERING.md.

**The rule pack is the test suite.** Every rule in the source grammar carries an
example, so each becomes a golden test automatically — roughly 200 for free,
growing as rules are added.

## 2026-08-11 (numbering and senses)

**The project defines its own rule numbering**, and everything refers to it:
`S-01`, `V-010`, `N-072`, `D-003`, `G-16`. The source document numbers rows per
table, so "rule 10" is ambiguous; karbon has a third unrelated scheme. One
canonical id ends the confusion.

Ids are **permanent, appended never inserted, and never reused**. Retired rules
keep their id with `active: false` so stored derivations and expert feedback
still resolve. Display order is a separate `sort_key`.

**The Rules screen is the canonical published reference** — all rules in
Persian, with `/rules/{id}` as a stable address the linguist can link to.

**Repeated dictionary senses are kept.** A word listed four times under the
same reading becomes one record, with `source_entry_ids` and `sense_count`
preserved, so the app can show "the dictionary lists this in 4 senses". Cheap
now, unrecoverable later. — project lead
